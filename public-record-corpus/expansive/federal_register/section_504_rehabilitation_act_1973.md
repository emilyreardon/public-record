================================================================================
SECTION 504, REHABILITATION ACT OF 1973
Public Law 93-112, enacted September 26, 1973
================================================================================

No otherwise qualified individual with a disability in the United States, as defined in section 705 of this title, shall, solely by reason of his or her disability, be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance.

================================================================================
CONTEXT: THE 1977 PROTEST AND THE SIGNING OF REGULATIONS
================================================================================

Section 504 was the first federal law that named disability as a civil rights category. Its language mirrored the Civil Rights Act of 1964. The regulations required to enforce it were not published from 1973 through 1977 -- no program receiving federal funds could be held to the law without them.

In 1977, the American Coalition of Citizens with Disabilities (ACCD) gave HEW Secretary Joseph A. Califano an ultimatum: sign unaltered 504 regulations by April or step down. When neither demand was met, ten protests launched simultaneously on April 5, 1977, outside regional HEW offices in San Francisco, Washington DC, Seattle, Los Angeles, Denver, Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta, New York City, Boston, and Philadelphia.

The only sit-in to continue past the first few days was at 50 UN Plaza in San Francisco, where approximately 100 people with disabilities occupied HEW offices for 26 days. The protest was sustained by broad coalition support, including the International Association of Machinists, Glide Memorial Church, and the local section of the Black Panther Party, which provided daily meals.

After the San Francisco sit-in reached national media coverage, Califano signed the 504 regulations on April 28, 1977. The regulations required curb cuts, handrails, ramps in federal buildings, access to public schools, and alternative language options including Braille and American Sign Language.

"In the face of government ignorance, and inhumanity, and deceit, and closed doors, we've persisted. Along with all of our beautiful supporters and we won the victory, by God nobody gave us anything!"
-- Kitty Cone, protest organizer, victory speech, April 28, 1977
